Community Meals

Privacy Policy

Last updated August 15, 2026

Community Meals ("the app") connects volunteers with food-relief organizations that cook and deliver meals. This policy explains what information the app collects, why, who can see it, and how to have it deleted. It covers the app itself; it does not speak on behalf of any individual organization using it.

Information the app collects

  • Account information. Email address and password, handled by our backend provider (Supabase) — the app itself never stores your password.
  • Profile information. Name, age (used only to determine whether the under-18 display rule below applies), and optionally a phone number and a profile photo.
  • Home address. Optional, and off by default. If you add one, it is used to sort opportunities by distance and, only if you separately turn on "share my address," to let a coordinator of your organization see the street address itself rather than just a distance. Your address is never shown to other volunteers, and never to any organization you have not joined.
  • Photos you upload. Documentation of a shift (cooking, packing, delivery) or a profile picture. Photos are stored in a private cloud bucket; every organization photo is tied to the shift it documents.
  • Participation records. Which shifts you sign up for, cancel, or complete; supply items you claim or purchase; messages you send in a shift's group chat; and notifications about shifts you are on.
  • Device calendar. If you choose to, the app can add a shift to your phone's own calendar. That entry lives on your device — the app only stores the calendar entry's ID, so it can remove the same entry later if you cancel.
  • Device notifications. Off by default. If you turn it on, the app can show a notification on your device when something in your feed changes (a delivery is confirmed, a shift you're on changes). This uses your device's own notification system, not a third-party push service, and nothing is sent anywhere until you turn it on.

What the app does not do

  • No advertising and no ad tracking.
  • No analytics or crash-reporting SDKs are built into the app.
  • No data is sold, ever.
  • Every image URL is a private, time-limited link — nothing is publicly hosted.

Who can see your information

The app is organized around volunteer organizations. If you join one, the following is visible to other members of that same organization — this is how a coordinator finds people to staff a shift, and how a group chat works:

  • Your name, phone number (if you added one), and profile photo.
  • Which shifts you have signed up for, and messages you send in that shift's chat.
  • Your approximate distance from a site, only if a coordinator looks up "who's nearby" — never your street address unless you separately turned on address sharing.

Volunteers under 18 are shown to everyone else by first name only, everywhere a name would otherwise appear — rosters, leaderboards, chat. This is the one age-based rule in the app; participation itself is identical regardless of age.

Members of a different organization on the same app never see your information. Organizations do not share rosters with each other.

Where your information is stored

The app is built on Supabase, which hosts the database and file storage this app uses. Every table is protected by row-level security rules enforced by the database itself, not just by the app's screens, so a request for data you are not entitled to is rejected at the source. Supabase is the only third party that processes your data on the app's behalf.

How long information is kept

  • Group chat messages are deleted automatically three days after the shift's date — whether or not the shift went ahead, and whether or not anyone asks. A scheduled job in the database does this; it is not something the app has to remember to run, and nobody can turn it off for a particular thread.
  • Everything else is kept for as long as your account exists. Your profile, participation records, photos you uploaded, and your notification history stay until you delete your account, which you can do at any time — see below.
  • Links to photos expire, the photos do not. Every image is served through a private link that stops working after an hour; the app generates a fresh one each time you view the photo.

Deleting your account

You can permanently delete your account from inside the app at any time — Profile → Danger zone → Delete my account. This immediately and permanently removes your profile, home address, notifications, chat messages, saved photo, and any upcoming (not yet completed) shift sign-ups. There is no recovery period and no way to undo it.

Two categories of information intentionally survive, with your name removed from them, so that an organization's operating history stays accurate for everyone else who relied on it: photos you contributed to a completed shift (now unattributed), and the record that a shift was delivered and by whom it was confirmed (also unattributed if that was you). Neither identifies you once your account is deleted.

Children

The app requires every volunteer to be at least 13 years old and does not knowingly collect information from anyone younger. Volunteers under 18 are shown to others by first name only, as described above.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes in a way that affects what is collected or who can see it, the "Last updated" date at the top of this page will change accordingly.
